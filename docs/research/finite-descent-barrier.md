# Finite descent near the canonical rational boundary

2026-09-11. Branch `research/proof-alternatives`, following ea7bc1c and
[CC.1--CC.4](coupled-cycle-descent.md).

The canonical condition at 2 does not repair a universal strategy of
choosing a finite torsor and excluding every twist by finitely many Brauer
classes. A general lifting theorem supplies a surviving twist above the
all-positive rational boundary. The argument below includes the required
local normalization and the almost-all-prime integrality condition.

This is a limitation on that proof template. It does not decide MSS3,
individual twists, the full possibly infinite Brauer group of an open
cover, or a genuine arithmetic descent. A potential decreasing integer
is isolated at the end; no map preserving the nine squares is supplied.

## FD.1. The imported rational-boundary lifting theorem

**CITED.** Let X be smooth, proper and geometrically integral over Q,
U a dense open, and Y -> U a torsor under a finite Q-group scheme G. For every P in X(Q),
some Q-twist of the desingularized normalization of X in Y has a rational
point above P. This allows ramification along X minus U and includes
noncommutative G.

Source: Julian Lawrence Demeio, *Ramified descent and transcendental
Brauer--Manin obstruction*, frozen arXiv:2112.00843v3 (7 May 2025),
[Theorem 3.2.1 and its setting](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.00843v3#S3.SS2).
The paper attributes the proof to Olivier Wittenberg. The source was read
on 2026-09-11; this note uses the numbering of that version, not an
unverified identification with the published theorem numbering.

The theorem gives a lift on a smooth proper model, not merely a possibly
singular point of the raw covering equations. It asserts existence of
**some** twist. It does not give a rational boundary point on every twist.

## FD.2. The canonical finite-descent/finite-Brauer barrier

Use GB's smooth full-square surface V, its admissible open U, and
P0=(u,v,r_1,...,r_8)=(0,0,1,...,1). Choose a smooth proper X containing
V, so P0 is still a smooth rational point. The canonical local domain is

\[
 \Omega_2=\{P\in U(\mathbb Q_2):r_j(P)\in1+4\mathbb Z_2\text{ for all }j\}.
\]

It is the intersection of U(Q_2) with a neighborhood of P0 in V(Q_2).
Every hypothetical primitive MSS3 has a signed-root lift in this domain
(UT.2). The domain is nonempty, but its points need not be rational.

**CITED DEDUCTION from FD.1 and GB.3.** For any finite-group torsor
pi:Y -> U there is a Q-twist and a geometrically integral component W of
that twist for which

\[
 \forall\text{ finite }B\subset\operatorname{Br}(W),\quad
 \{(Q_v)\in W(\mathbb A_{\mathbb Q})^B:
                  \pi(Q_2)\in\Omega_2\}\ne\varnothing.
\]

The twist and component can be chosen before B. Here B means a finite
subgroup, or the subgroup generated by finitely many torsion classes.

*Application and local audit.* Apply FD.1 to P0. It gives a smooth proper
model Z of a twist with a rational point R mapping to P0. Work in the
component containing R. Smooth geometric components are disjoint, and
the rational point fixes one of them; the connected component over Q
containing it is consequently geometrically integral. Its open W above
U is nonempty and dense. Finite normalization followed by desingularization
has no component supported only over the boundary.

Set B0=B intersect Br(Z). Include 2 in the finite exceptional prime set
used in GB.3. Near R at that place, continuity of pi ensures that W maps
into Omega_2. Local constancy lets us additionally require every
B0-evaluation to equal its value at R. A smooth rational point has local
points of the dense open W arbitrarily close at each completion.

At the other finitely many exceptional places choose points similarly
close to R. At all remaining primes choose points integral in a model of
W. Geometric integrality, Lang--Weil and Hensel give these after enlarging
the finite set; evaluations of the spread-out finite classes there vanish.
Reciprocity for their values at the rational point R gives orthogonality
to B0. Harari's formal lemma, exactly as in GB.3, corrects the remaining
classes away from the protected places and preserves the component at 2.
These are adeles of W, not just independently chosen local points.

The real component may also be kept near P0 with positive entry roots.
This is an optional local control, not an additional globally compatible
choice of root signs for every rational candidate.

### Quantifiers and limits

This is

\[
          \forall\pi\ \exists\sigma,W\ \forall B_{\rm finite}\ \exists(Q_v).
\]

It is not a single adelic point surviving every torsor and every class.
No nonemptiness of the full etale-Brauer set, or interchange of those
quantifiers, is asserted. The noncompact adelic space of an open variety
does not justify such an interchange.

A finite collection of torsors is covered by their product torsor;
finitely many classes on its factors can be pulled back together. A
finite adaptive tower also retains a branch: repeat the rational-boundary
lifting step on a smooth proper model of the surviving component. Thus
adding another finite cover cannot by itself close this proof template.

An independently justified arithmetic exclusion may remove that branch.
An integral or height condition that excludes a neighborhood of R is
also outside this argument, but must be shown necessary for every
candidate in its stated scope. Merely requiring the original roots to
be canonical at 2 is already included above.

## FD.3. Explicit square-root lifting along the base arc

**PROVEN, elementary specialization.** The current square-root covers
admit a direct version of the lifting construction. Use the formal arc

\[
 u=t,\quad v=3t,\quad r_{a,b}=\sqrt{1+(a+3b)t}\in\mathbb Q[[t]],
 \qquad r_{a,b}(0)=1.
\]

All nine square conditions hold formally. The distinctness polynomial
is -4200*t^8, hence the generic point lies in U. This is a Q((t)) point,
not a rational interior point of U.

For a nonzero Laurent series

\[
 f(t)=c\,t^e(1+h(t)),\quad c\in\mathbb Q^*,\quad e\in\mathbb Z,
 \quad h(t)\in t\mathbb Q[[t]],
\]

put d=sqclass(c) and t=s^2. Then

\[
 f(s^2)=d\left(s^e\sqrt{c/d}\sqrt{1+h(s^2)}\right)^2.
\]

The square root with constant coefficient 1 exists uniquely over Q.
If H(t)=sum h_n*t^n has h_0=1 and its square root has coefficients b_n,
then b_0=1 and

\[
 b_n=\frac{h_n-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}b_jb_{n-j}}2.
\]

One substitution t=s^2 works simultaneously for finitely many such
functions; each has its own constant twist d. Negative e gives a Laurent
root with a pole. The proper-model step is essential there: a pole in a
chosen affine cover coordinate does not eliminate the rational boundary.

For functions invertible on U the pullback along this arc is nonzero.
For a finite list of arbitrary rational functions, choose a rational
direction v=rho*t avoiding the finitely many zero leading homogeneous
forms of their numerators and denominators. The same argument then applies.
Repeated square-root stages can be handled by further substitutions of
the parameter; the general finite-torsor conclusion remains the cited FD.1.

The top-row half-sums and all four even-cycle slots have constant value 1
on the all-positive arc, so its twists are (1,1,1) and (1,1,1,1).
Their lift coordinates specialize to 1 and can be kept as 2-adic units
in FD.2. Thus the canonical unit-lift conditions of UT and CC do not
eliminate this particular surviving branch.

For comparison, (a-c)/2 has leading term -t/2, giving d=-2 and a root
of order one after t=s^2. The function 1/t gives d=1 and root s^(-1).
A prescribed twist d=17 for a unit with leading coefficient 1 fails:
1/17 has no rational square root. Parameter ramification does not erase
constant nonsquares. This is consistent with the selected GB.7 cover
having no rational all-equal fiber.

## FD.4. Twist-first-slot norm symbols cannot distinguish the local domain

**PROVEN.** On a CC cover every kappa_j is 1 mod 8. Therefore each is a
square in Q_2, including negative signed labels such as -7. If d is a
product of these constants times a rational square, then every quaternion
symbol (d,f) becomes split over Q_2(W), for any nonzero rational function f.
Use the square root of d to rescale its first generator, as in GB.1.
Its evaluation is zero at every Q_2 point where it is defined. Sums of
these classes, modulo constant Brauer classes, likewise have evaluations
independent of the local point.

This is a restriction on the first slots, not a statement about all
Brauer classes. For example (2,5) is nonsplit over Q_2: a norm equation
x^2-2y^2=5 would force x to be a unit and y integral by valuation parity,
whereas its left side is then 1 or 7 mod 8. It cannot be 5.

**CITED DEDUCTION using CC.4 and GB.3.** For every boundary-family twist
in CC.4, finite subgroups generated by twist-first-slot symbols (and
constants) cannot exclude the canonical domain at 2. Start with any
canonical local lift, which exists by CC.1. Its evaluations of classes
extending to the proper model equal the evaluations at the rational
boundary point at 2, since the nonconstant symbols split over Q_2.
Use boundary-near points at the other exceptional places, and apply
Harari's formal lemma preserving 2 as in FD.2.

This strengthens the limitation for that explicit infinite subfamily.
It does not exclude a different class detecting local conditions on an
individual nontrivial twist. Such a calculation would still leave the
general surviving-twist branch of FD.2 to be handled arithmetically.

## A concrete arithmetic step still missing

For the branch kappa_4=1, there is at least a natural smaller integer.
This observation is **PROVEN**, but it is **not a descent map**.

Take canonical primitive integer roots and suppose the corner class is
1. Then

\[
                       n^2=(ai+cg)/2
\]

has a positive integer root n: the right side is an odd integer and its
square class is 1. For an admissible grid, |ai|<m^2 and |cg|<m^2 by
the opposite-pair equations and U,V!=0. Hence 0<n<m, and n is odd.

Put x=(a+i)/2, y=(a-i)/2, z=(c+g)/2, w=(c-g)/2. Then all are integers,
and exactly

\[
 x^2=n^2+w^2,\quad z^2=n^2+y^2,\quad m^2=n^2+y^2+w^2.
\]

The other four roots must still satisfy

\[
 \begin{split}
 b^2&=m^2-2xy-2zw,& d^2&=m^2-2xy+2zw,\\
 f^2&=m^2+2xy-2zw,& h^2&=m^2+2xy+2zw.
 \end{split}
\]

The missing step is an explicit construction of another admissible full
grid with a smaller positive integer invariant, using these four extra
equations and, if needed, the remaining cycle conditions. Merely naming
n as a new center supplies none of its surrounding squares.

As a control, the known primitive degenerate AP grid with center m=5 has
canonical roots (5,-7,1,1,5,-7,-7,1,5), all four cycle labels 1, and n=3.
Thus the smaller number need not even satisfy the existing necessary
prime condition for a primitive center. A construction would have to
account for normalization and degeneracies, not silently reuse n.

This is the next concrete target within the cover track. The other
corner classes remain unbounded and would also require a universal
argument. Neither the smaller integer nor this case split proves MSS3.
