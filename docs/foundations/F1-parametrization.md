# F1 — The Lucas parametrization of 3×3 magic squares

**Status: PROVEN.** Verification: `python3 -m verify --only f1`.

This is the structural bedrock (Lucas 1876, reproved from scratch): 3×3 magic
squares form a 3-parameter linear family, and the parameters expose the
four-arithmetic-progression structure that everything else in this repository
builds on.

## Theorem F1.1 (parametrization)

Let $R$ be a commutative ring in which $3$ is not a zero divisor. A 3×3
matrix over $R$ is magic (all eight lines equal) **iff** it has the form

$$L(c,u,v) \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} c+u & c-u-v & c+v \\ c-u+v & c & c+u-v \\ c-v & c+u+v & c-u \end{pmatrix}$$

for some $c, u, v \in R$; the magic sum is then $S = 3c$, and $(c,u,v)$ is
uniquely determined by the matrix. Over $\mathbb{Z}$: a magic square has
$3 \mid S$, its center is $c = S/3$, and $u, v$ are the offsets of the
top-left and top-right entries from the center.

### Proof

**(⇐)** Each of the eight lines of $L(c,u,v)$ sums to $3c$ by inspection
(each line's $u$- and $v$-contributions cancel):
rows: $(c{+}u)+(c{-}u{-}v)+(c{+}v)$, $(c{-}u{+}v)+c+(c{+}u{-}v)$,
$(c{-}v)+(c{+}u{+}v)+(c{-}u)$; columns:
$(c{+}u)+(c{-}u{+}v)+(c{-}v)$, $(c{-}u{-}v)+c+(c{+}u{+}v)$,
$(c{+}v)+(c{+}u{-}v)+(c{-}u)$; diagonals: $(c{+}u)+c+(c{-}u)$,
$(c{+}v)+c+(c{-}v)$. All equal $3c$. ∎(⇐)

**(⇒)** Let $M = (a_{ij})$ be magic with sum $S$. The four lines through the
center (middle row, middle column, both diagonals) sum to $4S$; together they
cover the center entry four times and each of the other eight entries exactly
once, so

$$4S = \Big(\sum_{i,j} a_{ij}\Big) + 3a_{22} = 3S + 3a_{22},$$

using that the three rows sum to $3S$. Hence $S = 3a_{22}$: since $3$ is not
a zero divisor this pins $c := a_{22}$, $S = 3c$. Consequently **each line
through the center has its two outer entries summing to $2c$.**

Define $u := a_{11} - c$ and $v := a_{13} - c$. Then:
- main diagonal: $a_{33} = 2c - a_{11} = c - u$;
- anti-diagonal: $a_{31} = 2c - a_{13} = c - v$;
- top row: $a_{12} = 3c - a_{11} - a_{13} = c - u - v$;
- left column: $a_{21} = 3c - a_{11} - a_{31} = c - u + v$;
- right column: $a_{23} = 3c - a_{13} - a_{33} = c + u - v$;
- bottom row: $a_{32} = 3c - a_{31} - a_{33} = c + u + v$.

Every entry now matches $L(c,u,v)$; the remaining two lines (middle row,
middle column) were used only through the center identity and are re-verified
by (⇐). Uniqueness: $c, u, v$ are read off entries $a_{22}, a_{11}, a_{13}$.
∎

## Corollary F1.2 (the problem in Lucas coordinates)

An admissible magic square of squares (MSS3, [00-problem-statement.md](../00-problem-statement.md))
exists **iff** there are integers $c, u, v$ such that the nine numbers

$$c,\quad c\pm u,\quad c\pm v,\quad c\pm(u+v),\quad c\pm(u-v)$$

are distinct perfect squares. The center $c$ is itself a square, and the
magic sum is $3c$.

## Lemma F1.3 (distinctness in coordinates)

The nine entries of $L(c,u,v)$ are pairwise distinct **iff**

$$u\,v\,(u-v)\,(u+v)\,(u-2v)\,(u+2v)\,(2u-v)\,(2u+v) \;\neq\; 0 .$$

*Proof.* The nine offsets from $c$ are $0, \pm u, \pm v, \pm(u{+}v),
\pm(u{-}v)$. Two offsets coincide iff their difference vanishes; running over
all $\binom{9}{2}$ pairs, the differences that are not forced nonzero by the
others reduce (up to sign) to exactly the eight factors listed: $u$, $v$
(an offset equals $0$ or its own negative), $u\pm v$ (main pairs collide),
$u\pm 2v$ and $2u\pm v$ (a $\pm$ pair collides with a mixed offset, e.g.
$u = -(u-v) \Leftrightarrow 2u = v$). The verify script enumerates all 36
pairs and confirms each difference is $\pm$ one of the eight factors or a
unit multiple, so the product being nonzero is equivalent to all 36
differences being nonzero. ∎

**Remark (degenerate line).** If $v = 3u$ (or symmetrically $u = 3v$,
$v = -3u$, …), the offsets become $\{0, \pm u, \pm 2u, \pm 3u, \pm 4u\}$ —
nine squares in arithmetic progression, which is impossible by
[F3](F3-no-four-term-ap.md) once $u \ne 0$. Such collinear families are dead
on arrival; the surviving problem is "generic" $(u,v)$.

## Symmetries in coordinates

The $D_4$ action of Proposition 0.4 acts on $(c,u,v)$ by fixing $c$ and by
signed permutations of $(u,v)$ generated by $(u,v) \mapsto (v,u)$ (transpose,
i.e. reflect in the main diagonal) and $(u,v) \mapsto (-v,u)$ (rotation by
90°). Scaling acts by $(c,u,v) \mapsto (k^2c, k^2u, k^2v)$. Hence WLOG
normalizations such as $u > v > 0$ may be imposed on a putative MSS3 (after
excluding $uv(u^2-v^2)=0$ via F1.3).

## What the verify script proves mechanically

`verify/checks/f1_parametrization.py`:

1. **(⇐) as a polynomial identity.** Each of the eight line sums of
   $L(c,u,v)$ minus $3c$ is a polynomial of degree ≤ 1 in each of $c,u,v$; it
   vanishes on the grid $\{0,1\}^3$, and a multilinear polynomial vanishing
   on a full $\{0,1\}$-grid is identically zero (finite-difference /
   interpolation argument, stated in the script docstring). The script
   evaluates on $\{0,1,2\}^3$ for margin.
2. **(⇒) by exact linear algebra.** The magic conditions are 8 homogeneous
   linear equations in $(a_{11},\dots,a_{33},S)$. Exact Gaussian elimination
   over `fractions.Fraction` shows the solution space has dimension exactly
   3, and the three vectors $\partial L/\partial c$ (with $S=3$),
   $\partial L/\partial u$, $\partial L/\partial v$ (with $S=0$) are
   independent solutions — hence they span it, which **is** Theorem F1.1
   over $\mathbb{Q}$ (and over $\mathbb{Z}$ by the integrality of the
   read-off $c = a_{22}$, $u = a_{11}-c$, $v = a_{13}-c$).
3. **F1.3** by literal enumeration of all 36 offset differences.
4. **Anchor:** AB1 ([F6](F6-known-squares.md)) is recovered exactly by
   $L(c,u,v)$ with $(c,u,v) = (425^2,\, 373^2-425^2,\, 565^2-425^2)$.
