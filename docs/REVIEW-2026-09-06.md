# Independent review and proposed research program

Review of commit `cfd4513`, 2026-09-06. This is a code and mathematical-strategy review, not a certification of every written proof or historical computation. No existing mathematical claims or implementations were changed during the review.

The project has substantial mathematical structure and useful exact computation. Its immediate needs are a sound, independently replayable certification layer and attacks that retain the full conditions of a magic square. The most concrete new research opportunity found here is a bielliptic descent for the sixteen classes currently stopped at the curve C2.

## What has been achieved

The fundamental problem is represented correctly by the Lucas grid, with center m² and offsets U, V, U+V, U−V. After symmetry and distinctness conditions, all four must belong to the same congrua set D(m). The Gaussian-prime frame representation turns this into two simultaneous Laurent-polynomial relations with bounded exponent labels.

The latest progress is in A3 §§2.41–2.46 and research-log entry 116, rather than the older PROGRESS.md:

| Front | Repository's current result | What remains |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations | Lucas parametrization, congrua/Pythagorean dictionary, congruence restrictions, local solutions | These reductions do not exclude global rational points |
| One split prime | A3.6 excludes every exponent | Does not cover multiple independent split primes |
| Two split primes | Exclusions for shapes (1,1), (2,1), (3,1), (2,2), plus further documented families | No uniform theorem for arbitrary exponents; R_J remains open |
| Three primes, shape (1,1,1) | 2,944 classes: 1,492 dead, 1,452 finite; claimed finiteness up to scaling | Finite does not mean empty; no effective complete point lists |
| Three primes, shape (2,1,1) | Of 79,368 additional classes: 16,032 dead, 63,336 finite; claimed finiteness up to scaling | Same effectivity issue; these are incremental counts, not the complete box |
| Free frames | 3,900 formerly finite classes newly excluded; 3,576 through A3.8 | 4,230 weighted free-frame cases remain in that campaign |

| Searches and spheres | Additive desert through m = 10^7; explicit sphere-sieve laws and counterexamples to sieve totality | Bounded evidence; the known sieve family is insufficient |

These are the repository's recorded claims. The certification issues below prevent treating this review as an independent endorsement of all of them. The published background on the magic-square surface is [Bruin–Thomas–Várilly-Alvarado](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.08908).

The project explicitly chose written proofs and mechanical verification rather than Lean at kickoff. It is therefore a computational mathematics research program, not currently a proof-assistant formalization. A kernel-checked development would be a separate change of scope.

## Findings that should precede larger sweeps

### 1. Nonsquare constants can be discarded from a proof-critical curve

`compute/omega3.py::_disc_model` forms the squarefree discriminant from the nonconstant factors and drops `factor_list(disc)[0]`. `gp_model` also divides polynomial content without establishing that the content is a rational square. A nonsquare constant changes the quadratic twist and can change both rank and rational points. Related routes reuse these helpers or use similar normalization.

An exact counterexample, reproduced through the existing engine and PARI:

```python
import sympy as sp
from compute import omega3 as O

phi = 8425*O.th**2 - 11664*(O.tg**4 + 1)
assert phi.subs({O.tg: sp.Rational(4, 3),
                 O.th: sp.Rational(12, 5)}) == 0
assert O.is_frame_ratio(sp.Rational(4, 3))
assert O.is_frame_ratio(sp.Rational(12, 5))
print(O.decide_component(phi, 4, 2))
# Current result: dead, using the model y² = t⁴ + 1.
```

The point comes from the prime Pythagorean frames (3,4,5) and (5,12,13). The discriminant has squareclass 337, which cannot be removed. This demonstrates an unsound generic exclusion routine; the example is not itself a magic square or a recorded quadruple class.

**Scope of the audit:** 340 reconstructed genus-one discriminants were checked across representative committed direct/pullback mechanisms and all 72 one-step quotient kills in the (1,1,1) campaign. Their contents were all rational squares. No committed exclusion was demonstrated false. This sampling does not establish safety of every route or future input.

Required repair: retain the constant squareclass, carry an explicit map between each model and the original curve, and check the map by polynomial identities. Add this positive rational-point example and its twists to the exclusion module's controls. Audit every affected certificate before generalizing the engine.

### 2. Provisional genus verdicts survive a switch to strict mode

`compute/omega3.py::exact_genus_verdict` caches by polynomial and bidegrees, but the returned verdict also depends on `PROVISIONAL_FINITE` and computation settings. An isolated reproduction with mocked expensive arithmetic gives:

```text
provisional enabled -> finite, route=resolution-provisional
strict, same cache  -> finite, route=resolution-provisional
strict, fresh cache -> unknown
```

Several checks toggle provisional mode and restore settings without invalidating the cache. Cache evidence separately from the decision policy, or use an immutable computation context. Strict certification must inspect the evidence even on a cache hit. No historical theorem was shown false by this reproduction.

### 3. A green verification run does not imply full recertification

The PARI executable is hardcoded to one Windows user's path in `compute/pari_genus1.py`. CI runs Ubuntu and installs only SymPy/gmpy2. Several missing-PARI branches print a note and still PASS. Even FULL checks often validate stored counts and a sample: the (2,1,1) finiteness check recomputes 40 historical base loci and one resolved component.

The falsification gauntlet also has a coverage gap: after loading checks, its one registered predicate applies to none of its six anchor targets. The negative control tests the gauntlet infrastructure, but the actual exclusion mechanisms are not registered there.

Use separate statuses for artifact consistency, sampled recomputation, and complete certificate verification. Provide configurable PARI discovery and a pinned certification environment; missing required tools should make certification incomplete. Require the canonical classes in the certificate set to equal the classes produced by the enumerator. Add mechanism-specific positive controls, including rational points on twisted models and exceptional fibers.

### 4. Several mathematical summaries need correction

* A3 §2 calls the additive-desert conjecture weaker than nonexistence of a magic square. The implication goes the other way: no additive triple implies no required quadruple. The desert conjecture is the stronger target. An additive triple would not by itself solve the nine-square problem.
* ROADMAP R.11 says the base-locus half is done for every shape. A3 §2.41 explicitly retains the both-trinomial case, including possible common components. A uniform finiteness theorem still needs that exceptional case handled uniformly.
* A3 §2.44 gives genus J−1 to `3X^4 = y^(2J)+y^(2J−1)+1`. For its squarefree polynomial, the fourth-power cover has genus 3J−2 when J is odd and 3J−3 when J is even. J−1 belongs to the quadratic quotient. At J=5 the genera are 13 and 4 respectively. The squareclass condition needed to lift from the quotient must be retained.
* A3 §2.42 treats full rational 2-torsion as giving an untwisted Legendre model over Q. In general a quadratic-twist parameter is also needed; lambda alone does not identify the arithmetic curve. The table itself contains different twists sharing lambda.
* PROGRESS.md still says 124 checks and log entries 1–44; the registry now has 199 checks and the log reaches 116. README's description of `compute/` as not proof-critical no longer matches the imports and proof dependencies.

## Priority research proposal: descend C2 while retaining the square coordinate

This is a new proposed route derived and tested during this review, not a completed exclusion.

A3 §2.45 reduces sixteen classes to

```text
C2: y² = A(s) B(s)
A(s) = 25s³ − 61s² + 43s + 1
B(s) = 25s³ − 29s² + 11s + 1.
```

The original classes require a square coordinate after their recorded rescalings/inversions. All three scale factors are rational squares:

```text
1 = 1²,   56644 = 238²,   42025/1849 = (205/43)².
```

Thus the relevant affine points must lift to `H: y² = A(t²)B(t²)`. Solving all of C2(Q), whose Jacobian appears generic, is unnecessary if the square-coordinate subset can be determined directly.

There is a useful elementary descent. Write t=a/b in lowest terms and define

```text
F = 25a⁶ − 61a⁴b² + 43a²b⁴ + b⁶
G = 25a⁶ − 29a⁴b² + 11a²b⁴ + b⁶.
```

Both are positive: `A(s)=s(5s−7)²+(3s−1)²` and `B(s)=s(5s−3)²+(s+1)²` for s≥0. Their common prime divisors are only 2 and 5. Indeed `F−G=−32a²b²(a²−b²)`; coprimality of a,b excludes other common primes, including those dividing a²−b² by substitution. Their common valuations are:

* at 2: zero for opposite parity, exactly 3 if both a,b are odd (both forms are 8 modulo 16);
* at 5: zero if 5 does not divide b, exactly 2 otherwise. For b=5k, the two forms divided by 25 reduce to a⁴(a²−k²) and a⁴(a²+k²) modulo 5, so they cannot both acquire another factor of 5.

Consequently gcd(F,G) is one of 1, 8, 25, 200. If FG is a square, the two forms have the same squareclass δ in {1,2}. Every relevant point therefore maps to one of just two curves:

```text
Gδ: z² = δ(25t⁶ − 29t⁴ + 11t² + 1),   δ = 1 or 2.
```

Each Gδ is a smooth bielliptic genus-2 curve. Its two elliptic quotients have models `v²=δ B(s)` and `w²=δ s B(s)`, with maps `(t,z) -> (t²,z)` and `(t,z) -> (t²,tz)`. The resulting Jacobian splits up to isogeny over Q.

Direct PARI computations during this review, retaining the twist constants, gave matching lower and upper rank bounds:

| δ | Quotient from B(s) | Rank | Quotient from sB(s) | Rank |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | 1840d1 | 1 | 184b1 | 1 |
| 2 | 7360r1 | 1 | 1472a1 | 1 |

Thus these genus-2 Jacobians have rank 2 and known extra endomorphisms from their elliptic factors. They fit the specific setting of [Bianchi–Padurariu's bielliptic quadratic Chabauty method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11635), with [author-maintained SageMath code](https://github.com/bianchifrancesca/QC_bielliptic). This reopens a method that the generic C2 quotient makes look unavailable. A good ordinary prime, suitable generators, local-height computation, precision control, and a final Mordell–Weil sieve/completeness argument still have to be supplied; the Sage code alone is not a complete proof.

PARI `hyperellratpoints` with bound 10,000 returned only the affine points `(0,±1)` for G1 and `(±1,±4)` for G2. G1 also has two rational points at infinity; G2 has none. These are bounded search results, not complete point lists. If completeness is proved, they give only s=0,1,∞ on C2, and the recorded exceptional-fiber and frame-lift checks can be independently replayed to exclude the sixteen classes.

**Acceptance criterion:** a complete point computation for both Gδ, a checked covering argument for all sixteen records, and explicit handling of t=∞ and every denominator-zero/singular exception. This is a focused partial result, not a solution of the whole (1,1,1) box.

Reproduce the elliptic rank computations directly in PARI:

```gp
B = 25*x^3 - 29*x^2 + 11*x + 1;
for(d=1,2, for(k=0,1,
  E=ellinit(ellfromeqn(y^2-d*x^k*B));
  print([d,k,ellrank(E),ellidentify(E)[1][1]])
));
for(d=1,2, print([d,hyperellratpoints(d*subst(B,x,x^2),10000)]));
```

## Other ambitious workstreams, in order

1. **Generalize free-frame elimination into a theorem about supported label patterns.** Extend `omega3_freeframe.py` and the two-frame ladder around the weighted relation `2e(B) ± e(C) ± e(D)=0`, retaining distinctness and the free-frame lift. First classify the remaining 4,230 cases by normalized relation and actual twist; do not assume an unrestricted weighted relation is impossible. Seek a finite collection of symbolic reductions valid across exponents. Success is a theorem excluding an infinite family of labels, not another large tally.

2. **Prove uniform three-prime finiteness with all exceptions stated.** Use `omega3_minors.py`, `omega3_resolve.py`, and `omega3_finiteness.py` to derive genus bounds from exponent support/Newton polygons and a complete singularity correction. Treat low-genus components and the both-trinomial elimination base loci separately. Test proposed bounds on strategically selected (3,1,1) classes outside the two existing boxes before a full sweep. The target is finiteness for every fixed exponent shape (a,b,c); this still does not imply finiteness over the union of all shapes, or emptiness of any finite set.

3. **Attack arbitrary prime support through both relations simultaneously.** A proof of the full problem needs either a uniform arithmetic descent, an effective global height/support bound, or a verified construction. The high-risk experiment is to encode prime valuations across all four labels and both equations and seek a minimal-counterexample reduction: a removable frame, a proper torsion-coset relation, or a strictly decreasing height. Dense-support survivors are the test cases. Merely counting S-unit solutions for a fixed prime set does not control all prime sets, and higher-dimensional resultant varieties cannot be dispatched by Faltings' theorem for curves.

4. **Keep the surface program as a distinct geometry objective.** Classify the algebraic integral curves of the explicit quartic differential, including its singular/exceptional behavior, rather than endlessly extending low-degree scans. A complete rational-curve classification would be valuable and could settle a function-field statement under its stated field hypotheses. It still needs an additional arithmetic theorem to exclude isolated rational points. Picard/Brauer computations should target explicit covers and twist conditions, not be described as an automatic route from quasi-hyperbolicity to nonexistence.

## Architecture needed to support those goals

The important deepening opportunity is a certified-curve module: model transformations, twist constants, exceptional fibers, genus evidence and rational-point completeness should have locality inside one checked implementation. Discovery engines can propose certificates; an independent checker should validate them through a small interface. This creates leverage across free-frame elimination, quotient towers and genus campaigns, and gives tests meaningful mathematical contracts.

A second module should own immutable run settings, canonical class enumeration and artifact provenance. Per-class evidence should include source commit, dependency versions, normalized input, exact transformations and any external theorem assumptions. Derive summary counts and documentation from that ledger. This addresses the current mixture of mutable globals, sampled checks, abbreviated artifact verdicts and manually maintained totals.

The immediate order is: repair and audit certification; execute the two-curve bielliptic descent; generalize the successful mechanism; then spend substantial compute on new shapes selected to test a uniform theorem.
