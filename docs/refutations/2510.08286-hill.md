**Subject.** O. Hill, *"On Arithmetic Progressions and a Proof of the
Nonexistence of Magic Squares of Squares"*, arXiv:2510.08286 (math.GM;
v1 2025-10-09, v2 2025-10-19, v3 2026-04-07). Its Theorem 3.1 claims:
*it is impossible to construct a 3×3 magic square consisting solely of
square integers* — the long-open problem (LaBar 1984; Guy D15; Gardner
1996).

**Result.** The proof of Theorem 3.1 is invalid. Its penultimate
equation, eq. (29), is proven here to be a positive multiple of the
paper's own Lemma-3.2 constraint — an algebraic identity carrying no
Diophantine information (Theorem 1) — and the final inference drawn
from (29), namely $\beta_{1d}^2 - \beta_{1n}^2 = 0$ "since the
coefficients of odd powers of $\alpha_{1d}$ are 0," fails on an
explicit configuration that satisfies every hypothesis and every
positivity condition the inference invokes (Theorem 2). The theorem's
*statement* — the open problem — is untouched by this note: we refute
the proof, not the claim.

*This document is self-contained: all of the paper's notation is
recalled, both theorems can be checked by hand, and §7 contains a
~40-line sympy script that verifies everything mechanically. Status
tags (per this repository's protocol): Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 are
PROVEN, machine-verified by `verify/checks/a1_eq29.py` (exact integer
polynomial arithmetic and exact arithmetic in
$\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{105961})$, standard library only) and independently
by `compute/hill_literal_controls.py` (the paper's definitions
implemented verbatim, 60-digit precision). Analysis first completed
2026-08-28; see [A1](../attacks/A1-hill-audit.md) §7 for the full
audit trail.*

## 1. The paper's framework, recalled

For integers (or reals) $0 < p < q < r$ with
$$q^2 - p^2 \;=\; r^2 - q^2 \;=\; \Sigma\mathcal{P},$$
the paper calls the corresponding pair of consecutive runs of odd
numbers an **AP pair** $\mathcal{P}$ with sum $\Sigma\mathcal{P}$, and
associates to it (its §2):

- lengths $\mathcal{P}^{n_1} = q - p$, $\mathcal{P}^{n_2} = r - q$, and
  $\kappa = \mathcal{P}^{n_1}/\mathcal{P}^{n_2} > 1$;
- $\alpha = \kappa(\kappa+1)/(\kappa-1)$, written $\alpha_n/\alpha_d$
  (eq. (9)); offsets $\mathcal{P}^1 = 2p$, $\mathcal{P}^2 = 2q$,
  $\mathcal{P}^3 = 2r - 2$;
- $s = \sqrt{(\alpha_n - 3\alpha_d)^2 - 8\alpha_d^2}$ and $N$ with
  $N(N + 2s) = 8\alpha_d^2$, i.e. a root of
  $N^2 - 2(\alpha_n - 3\alpha_d)N + 8\alpha_d^2 = 0$
  (eqs. (10)–(12); both roots satisfy (12), and (13)/(14) both recover
  $\kappa$).

Useful consequences of these definitions (each is a two-line
computation, and each is machine-verified at every configuration used
below): $\Sigma\mathcal{P} = \alpha\,(\mathcal{P}^{n_2})^2$ (his (6)),
$$q = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{P}^{n_2}(\alpha - 1)
\quad\text{(his (8))}, \qquad
p = \frac{\mathcal{P}^{n_2}\, s}{2\alpha_d}
\quad\text{(equivalent to his (7))}, \qquad
s = \frac{8\alpha_d^2 - N^2}{2N}. \tag{$\ast$}$$

For a hypothetical 3×3 magic square of squares, the paper (its §3)
derives three **distinct** AP pairs $\mathcal{P}_1, \mathcal{P}_2,
\mathcal{P}_3$ with equal sums $D_1$ (his (17)–(20)), sets
$$\beta_1 = \frac{\mathcal{P}_1^{n_2}}{\mathcal{P}_2^{n_2}}
 = \sqrt{\frac{\alpha_2}{\alpha_1}}, \qquad
\beta_2 = \frac{\mathcal{P}_2^{n_2}}{\mathcal{P}_3^{n_2}}
 = \sqrt{\frac{\alpha_3}{\alpha_2}}$$
(his (21)–(22)), splits them as $\beta_{1n} = \sqrt{\alpha_{2n}/\alpha_{1n}}$,
$\beta_{1d} = \sqrt{\alpha_{2d}/\alpha_{1d}}$, $\beta_{2n} =
\sqrt{\alpha_{3n}/\alpha_{2n}}$, $\beta_{2d} = \sqrt{\alpha_{3d}/\alpha_{2d}}$
— *"where $\beta_{1n}, \beta_{1d}, \beta_{2n}, \beta_{2d} \in
\mathbb{R}^+$"* (his (23)–(26), p. 5) — and proves (Lemma 3.2, whose
case analysis is correct) the spacing constraint
$$\Sigma\mathbf{p}_A = \Sigma\mathbf{p}_B, \quad\text{i.e.}\quad
E := (p_2^2 - q_1^2) - (p_3^2 - q_2^2) = 0. \tag{L3.2}$$

From these ingredients the paper asserts its eq. (29):
$$N_3^2\beta_{2n}^2\beta_{2d}^2\bigl(8\beta_{1d}^4\alpha_{1d}^2 - N_2^2\bigr)^2
 - N_2^2\bigl(8\beta_{2d}^4\beta_{1d}^4\alpha_{1d}^2 - N_3^2\bigr)^2
 = 4N_2^2N_3^2\beta_{1n}^2\beta_{1d}^2\beta_{2n}^2\beta_{2d}^2(\alpha_{1n}-\alpha_{1d})^2
 - 4N_2^2N_3^2\beta_{2n}^2\beta_{2d}^2\bigl(\beta_{1n}^2\alpha_{1n}-\beta_{1d}^2\alpha_{1d}\bigr)^2
\tag{29}$$
and concludes (p. 7, quoted in full in §5 below) that since the LHS
has only even powers of $\alpha_{1d}$, the odd-power coefficients of
the RHS must vanish, forcing $\beta_{1d}^2 - \beta_{1n}^2 = 0$, hence
$\beta_1 = 1$, hence $\mathcal{P}_1 = \mathcal{P}_2 = \mathcal{P}_3$ —
contradicting distinctness.

## 2. Preliminary: what is correct in the paper

Fairness requires saying what checks out, and it is most of the paper:

1. **The encoding is faithful and complete.** The constraint set
   {three equal-sum AP pairs} + {(L3.2)} is *equivalent* to: the nine
   values form a 3×3 additive grid $M + iD + jF$, $(i,j) \in
   \{0,1,2\}^2$ — which is exactly the classical Lucas structure of a
   3×3 magic square. (Machine-verified, `a1.hill_grid`; the grid
   arranges into the magic square via a pair of orthogonal Latin
   squares.) Nothing is lost in the reduction. A corollary worth
   stating: *no integer counterexample to the paper's hypotheses can
   exist* — nine distinct integer squares satisfying them would **be**
   a magic square of squares — so any flaw must be inferential.
2. **The algebra is correct through (30).** Equation (29) is a genuine
   consequence of the framework (Theorem 1 below says precisely which
   consequence), the case analysis of Lemma 3.2 is sound, and the
   printed (30) with its bracket is the correct expansion of the RHS
   (the RHS factors as $4N_2^2N_3^2\beta_{2n}^2\beta_{2d}^2
   (\beta_{1d}^2-\beta_{1n}^2)(\beta_{1n}^2\alpha_{1n}^2 -
   \beta_{1d}^2\alpha_{1d}^2)$, and the bracket is the second factor
   expanded via his (12); both verified exactly).

The error is confined to the single inference after (30).

## 3. Theorem 1 — equation (29) is the constraint (L3.2) in costume

Write $b_{1n} = \beta_{1n}^2,\ b_{1d} = \beta_{1d}^2,\ b_{2n} =
\beta_{2n}^2,\ b_{2d} = \beta_{2d}^2$ (every $\beta$ enters (29)
squared), and $n = \alpha_{1n}$, $d = \alpha_{1d}$, so that
$\alpha_{2d} = b_{1d}d$, $\alpha_{3d} = b_{2d}b_{1d}d$, $\alpha_{2n} =
b_{1n}n$.

**Theorem 1.** *As polynomials in
$\mathbb{Z}[n, d, N_2, N_3, b_{1n}, b_{1d}, b_{2n}, b_{2d}]$,*
$$\mathrm{LHS}(29) - \mathrm{RHS}(29)
\;=\; K_0 \cdot \frac{4E}{t^2}, \qquad
K_0 := 4N_2^2N_3^2\,b_{1d}^2\,b_{2d}^2\,d^2
\;=\; 4N_2^2N_3^2\,\beta_{1d}^4\beta_{2d}^4\,\alpha_{1d}^2,$$
*where $t = \mathcal{P}_3^{n_2}$ is the free scale and $4E/t^2$ is the
constraint (L3.2) written in the same variables via the identities
$(\ast)$ and (21)–(26), with denominators cleared.*

*Proof (checkable by hand).* By $(\ast)$ and the $\beta$-substitutions,
with $t := \mathcal{P}_3^{n_2}$, $\mathcal{P}_2^{n_2} = \beta_2 t$,
$\mathcal{P}_1^{n_2} = \beta_1\beta_2 t$:
$$\frac{4E}{t^2} = T_1 - T_2 - T_3 + T_4, \qquad
\begin{aligned}
T_1 = \tfrac{4p_2^2}{t^2} &= \frac{b_{2n}}{b_{2d}}\cdot
  \frac{(8b_{1d}^2d^2 - N_2^2)^2}{4N_2^2\,b_{1d}^2d^2}, &
T_2 = \tfrac{4q_1^2}{t^2} &= \frac{b_{1n}b_{2n}}{b_{1d}b_{2d}}\cdot
  \frac{(n-d)^2}{d^2},\\
T_3 = \tfrac{4p_3^2}{t^2} &= \frac{(8b_{2d}^2b_{1d}^2d^2 - N_3^2)^2}
  {4N_3^2\,b_{2d}^2b_{1d}^2d^2}, &
T_4 = \tfrac{4q_2^2}{t^2} &= \frac{b_{2n}}{b_{2d}}\cdot
  \frac{(b_{1n}n - b_{1d}d)^2}{b_{1d}^2d^2}.
\end{aligned}$$
Multiplying each term by $K_0$ gives, term for term,
$$K_0T_1 = N_3^2\,b_{2n}b_{2d}\,(8b_{1d}^2d^2 - N_2^2)^2, \qquad\quad
K_0T_3 = N_2^2\,(8b_{2d}^2b_{1d}^2d^2 - N_3^2)^2,$$
$$K_0T_2 = 4N_2^2N_3^2\,b_{1n}b_{1d}b_{2n}b_{2d}\,(n-d)^2, \qquad
K_0T_4 = 4N_2^2N_3^2\,b_{2n}b_{2d}\,(b_{1n}n - b_{1d}d)^2,$$
which are precisely the four terms of (29) with matching signs:
$K_0(T_1 - T_3) = \mathrm{LHS}(29)$ and $K_0(T_2 - T_4) =
\mathrm{RHS}(29)$. $\blacksquare$

**Corollary 1.1.** *Since $K_0 > 0$ on every configuration, eq. (29)
is equivalent to the constraint (L3.2). In particular:*
1. *(29) holds on **every** configuration satisfying the paper's
   framework — including all real ones — and fails the moment (L3.2)
   is broken. It carries no arithmetic information beyond (L3.2).*
2. *The entire derivation of §§2–3 of the paper, through (30), consists
   of identities valid over $\mathbb{R}$. Over $\mathbb{R}$ the
   paper's hypothesis set is abundantly solvable (any nine distinct
   positive reals in the grid pattern of §2.1 are squares of reals),
   with $\beta_1 \ne 1$ generically. Hence no inference valid over
   $\mathbb{R}$ can complete the proof from (29).*

## 4. Theorem 2 — the explicit counterexample to the final inference

**Theorem 2.** *There is a configuration satisfying every hypothesis
and every positivity condition invoked by the paper's final step, on
which eq. (29) holds with both sides nonzero and $\beta_1 = 6/5 \ne
1$. Consequently the inference "(29) $\Rightarrow \beta_{1d}^2 -
\beta_{1n}^2 = 0$" is invalid.*

**The witness.** Take the 3×3 additive grid $M + iD + jF$ with
$(M, D, F) = (4, 3360, 2112)$ — a genuine magic square (magic constant
16428, center $5476 = 74^2$) with **six** perfect-square entries:
$$\begin{pmatrix} 94^2 & 2^2 & 7588 \\ 4228 & 74^2 & 82^2 \\
58^2 & 10948 & 46^2 \end{pmatrix}.$$
Its three AP pairs, in the paper's formalism:

| | $(p, q, r)$ | $\kappa$ | $\alpha = \alpha_n/\alpha_d$ | $s$ | $N$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\mathcal{P}_1$ | $(2,\ 58,\ 82)$ | $7/3$ | $35/6$ | $1$ | $16$ |
| $\mathcal{P}_2$ | $(46,\ 74,\ 94)$ | $7/5$ | $42/5$ | $23$ | $4$ |
| $\mathcal{P}_3$ | $(\sqrt{4228},\ \sqrt{7588},\ \sqrt{10948})$ | — | rep. $\alpha_{3d} := 5$ | see below | see below |

- $\mathcal{P}_1, \mathcal{P}_2$ are **fully integral** AP pairs with
  common sum (congruum) $D_1 = 3360$, coprime representations, and all
  of the paper's (6)–(8), (11)–(14) satisfied in $\mathbb{Z}$.
- $\mathcal{P}_3$ is **forced by (L3.2) itself**: $p_3^2 = q_2^2 +
  p_2^2 - q_1^2 = 5476 + 2116 - 3364 = 4228$, $q_3^2 = 7588$, $r_3^2 =
  10948$ (none a perfect square — as §2.1 guarantees must happen for
  some pair). Exactly, in $\mathbb{Q}(g)$ with $g = \sqrt{105961}$:
  $t^2 = (r_3 - q_3)^2 = 18536 - 56g > 0$, $\alpha_3 = 3360/t^2 =
  (2317 + 7g)/420$, $s_3^2 = (5\alpha_3 - 15)^2 - 200 = 1057(331 +
  g)/504 > 0$, $N_3 = (5\alpha_3 - 15) - s_3 > 0$ (product of the two
  $N_3$-roots $= 8\alpha_{3d}^2 = 200$), $\beta_{2n}^2 = 5\alpha_3/42
  > 0$, $\beta_{2d}^2 = 1$.
- The interleaving is the paper's own Lemma-3.2 **case (c)**:
  $\mathcal{P}_2^1 = 92 < 116 = \mathcal{P}_1^2$, $\mathcal{P}_3^1 =
  2\sqrt{4228} < 148 = \mathcal{P}_2^2$, and the $<\!\|$ relations
  hold ($2\sqrt{4228} \le 186$, $92 \le 162$); his case-(c) formulas
  give $\Sigma\mathbf{p}_A = \Sigma\mathbf{p}_B = 1248$ on the nose.

**Verification of the theorem.** (L3.2) holds by construction (an
integer identity: $46^2 - 58^2 = 4228 - 74^2 = -1248$), so by Theorem
1, eq. (29) **holds** at the witness. Its two sides are equal to
$$4N_2^2N_3^2\beta_{2n}^2\beta_{2d}^2\,
(\beta_{1d}^2 - \beta_{1n}^2)(\beta_{1n}^2\alpha_{1n}^2 -
\beta_{1d}^2\alpha_{1d}^2) \;=\; -298392.5892\ldots \;\ne\; 0,$$
every factor being nonzero: $N_2^2 = 16$, $N_3^2 \ne 0$,
$\beta_{2n}^2\beta_{2d}^2 > 0$, and — the point —
$$\beta_{1d}^2 - \beta_{1n}^2 = \frac{5}{6} - \frac{6}{5}
 = -\frac{11}{30} \ne 0, \qquad
\beta_{1n}^2\alpha_{1n}^2 - \beta_{1d}^2\alpha_{1d}^2
 = \tfrac{6}{5}\cdot 35^2 - \tfrac{5}{6}\cdot 6^2 = 1440 \ne 0,$$
with $\beta_1 = 24/20 = 6/5$. Meanwhile every positivity the final
step cites ($N_1, N_2, N_3, \beta_{1n}, \beta_{1d}, \beta_{2n},
\beta_{2d} > 0$) holds. The step's hypotheses are satisfied; its
conclusion is false. $\blacksquare$

*(Robustness: the representation choice for $\mathcal{P}_3$ is
immaterial — $\alpha_{3d} = 1$ in place of $5$ rescales the
$\mathcal{P}_3$-quantities and (29) still holds with both sides
$-477.428\ldots \ne 0$; and in a sweep of 500 random real grids, (29)
held on every one with $\beta_1$ ranging over $[1.0008, 3.998]$ and
never equal to $1$.)*

## 5. The invalid step, on its own terms

The paper's complete final argument (p. 7):

> *"Compare this with the LHS of equation (29). Clearly, this is a
> quadratic in $\alpha_{1d}^2$ and, as such, the coefficients of odd
> powers of $\alpha_{1d}$ are 0, which must also hold in the RHS.
> Obviously, $N_1, N_2, N_3, \beta_{1n}, \beta_{1d}, \beta_{2n},
> \beta_{2d} > 0$, as per the definitions in equations (11), (21) and
> (22), so $\beta_{1d}^2 - \beta_{1n}^2 = 0$."*

There are exactly two readings, and each fails:

- **Numeric reading.** For any given (hypothetical) magic square,
  (29) is one equation between two *numbers* — $\alpha_{1d}$ is a
  single fixed integer, and $N_1, N_2, N_3$ and the $\beta$'s are
  determined by the same data (indeed the paper's own (11)–(12)
  define $N_1$ from $\alpha_{1n}, \alpha_{1d}$, so the RHS's
  "coefficients" $16\beta_{1n}^2/N_1^2$, $24\beta_{1n}^2/N_1,\ldots$
  are themselves functions of $\alpha_{1d}$). Numbers do not have
  coefficients: the even and odd parts of the two sides of a numeric
  equality need not separately agree, any more than they do in
  $3 + 4 = 7$. At the witness, the "odd part" of (30) that the
  argument declares must vanish evaluates to $-152180.22\ldots$
- **Polynomial reading.** If instead all eight quantities are treated
  as free variables so that coefficient comparison would be
  meaningful, the premise fails: by Theorem 1, $\mathrm{LHS}(29) -
  \mathrm{RHS}(29)$ is **not** the zero polynomial (it is $K_0$ times
  the constraint polynomial), so (29) is not a polynomial identity
  and there is nothing to compare coefficients of.

There is no third reading: any hypothetical "variation of
$\alpha_{1d}$" drags $N_1$ and the $\beta$'s along with it, so the
"coefficients" are not well-defined constants under any
interpretation.

**No local repair is possible.** One might hope integrality could
rescue the step (the witness's $\mathcal{P}_3$ is irrational). It
cannot: (i) the step justifies itself by positivity alone (quoted
above), and every positivity it invokes holds at the witness, whose
$\alpha_{1d} = 6$ — the comparison variable itself — comes from a
fully integral pair; (ii) the paper itself places $\beta_{1n},
\beta_{1d}, \beta_{2n}, \beta_{2d}$ in $\mathbb{R}^+$, and they enter
(29) only squared; (iii) decisively, restricting the step's validity
to all-rational configurations is circular — by §2(1) the
all-rational solution set being empty *is the theorem being proved*,
and a step that is sound only if the conclusion is true proves
nothing. By Corollary 1.1(2), any correct completion must inject a
genuinely new Diophantine argument; the paper contains none (its
integrality side conditions — coprime $\alpha_n/\alpha_d$,
perfect-square discriminant — are never used by the derivation, as
Theorem 1 shows).

## 6. Scope

This note refutes the *proof*, not the *statement*: whether a 3×3
magic square of distinct integer squares exists remains open, in both
directions. A byproduct worth keeping: the witness construction
generalizes — any two integer three-term APs of squares with a common
difference $D$ and $q_2^2 + p_2^2 - q_1^2 > 0$ yield a magic square
with six square entries — a clean near-miss family produced by the
paper's own (correct) framework.

## 7. Mechanical verification

Self-contained (requires only [sympy](https://www.sympy.org); runs in
seconds; also stored as
[`compute/hill_refutation_standalone.py`](../../compute/hill_refutation_standalone.py)):

```python
import sympy as sp

n, d, N2, N3, b1n, b1d, b2n, b2d = sp.symbols(
    'n d N2 N3 b1n b1d b2n b2d', positive=True)

# eq. (29), literally as printed (v3, p. 6); b** = beta_**^2:
LHS = N3**2*b2n*b2d*(8*b1d**2*d**2 - N2**2)**2 \
    - N2**2*(8*b2d**2*b1d**2*d**2 - N3**2)**2
RHS = 4*N2**2*N3**2*b1n*b1d*b2n*b2d*(n - d)**2 \
    - 4*N2**2*N3**2*b2n*b2d*(b1n*n - b1d*d)**2

# the Lemma-3.2 constraint 4E/t^2 in the same variables (Theorem 1 text):
a2d, a3d = b1d*d, b2d*b1d*d
T1 = (b2n/b2d) * (8*a2d**2 - N2**2)**2/(4*N2**2) / a2d**2
T2 = (b1n*b2n/(b1d*b2d)) * (n - d)**2 / d**2
T3 = (8*a3d**2 - N3**2)**2/(4*N3**2) / a3d**2
T4 = (b2n/b2d) * (b1n*n - b1d*d)**2 / a2d**2
E4 = T1 - T2 - T3 + T4

# THEOREM 1:
assert sp.simplify((LHS - RHS) - 4*N2**2*N3**2*b1d**2*b2d**2*d**2*E4) == 0

# THEOREM 2 (the witness; exact):
t2 = 18536 - 56*sp.sqrt(105961)
alpha3 = sp.Rational(3360, 1)/t2
vals = {n: 35, d: 6, N2: 4,
        b1n: sp.Rational(6, 5), b1d: sp.Rational(5, 6),
        b2n: 5*alpha3/42, b2d: 1,
        N3: (5*alpha3 - 15) - sp.sqrt((5*alpha3 - 15)**2 - 200)}
lhs_v, rhs_v = LHS.subs(vals), RHS.subs(vals)
assert sp.simplify(lhs_v - rhs_v) == 0   # (29) HOLDS at the witness
assert abs(sp.N(lhs_v, 30)) > 1          # ... with both sides NONZERO
print("both sides of (29) =", sp.N(lhs_v, 30), "; beta1^2 = 36/25 != 1")
```
